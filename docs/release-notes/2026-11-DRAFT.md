# Omarchy Mobile 2026.MM.DD (developer preview)

The first release of Omarchy Mobile: native Omarchy (Arch Linux ARM, Hyprland and Omarchy's own packages) on the Google Pixel 7 Pro, with a mainline Linux kernel and no Android underneath. This is a **developer preview**: it installs and runs as an everyday Linux phone for people comfortable with fastboot, but some hardware is still being worked on (below), installing erases the phone's data, and stock Android stays on slot A as the way back.

Omarchy Mobile is a community project built on [Omarchy](https://omarchy.org). It is not an official Omarchy or Basecamp product, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google.

## What's in it

- **Device:** Google Pixel 7 Pro (`cheetah`, Tensor G2 / gs201). No other phone is part of this release.
- **Kernel:** `kernel-2026.11.01`, linux-omarchy-mobile's `omamobile` branch at `2219cc6cda77` (Linux 7.3-rc6 plus our drivers); `uname -r` is `7.3.0-rc6-omamobile-g2219cc6cda77`. The source is public at that tag before any binary ships.
- **Userspace:** Arch Linux ARM, Omarchy from its stable channel with our pending patches (re-applied after every Omarchy upgrade), and the signed `[omamobile]` repository (pkgs.omamobile.org, stable channel) for the phone shell, Mesa for the Mali-G710, Hyprland, libcamera and the device support packages.
- **Updates:** `omarchy update` on the phone, from the stable channel, kernel included: the kernel is the `linux-omamobile` package, and `omamobile-boot-update`'s pacman hook writes a new one to slot B (it never reboots by itself; the update sheet asks for a restart). Both are in this release's root image, and the release was tested with a kernel update through them before it shipped.

### New in the last weeks

- **Phone, Contacts and Messages** come in the image: Omarchy Mobile's own apps for the dialpad, call log, favorites and contacts (with import and export), and SMS conversations with drafts and search. With the modem up they place and take calls and send and receive texts (below); without it a call is refused with "No modem is available" and a text you send is kept, marked "Not sent", to retry later.
- **The modem, on by default:** once `tools/phone-provision` has put Google's modem files, your backup's checksums and the phone's board id on the phone, the modem stack starts at every boot on its own (`omamobile-modem-boot.target`: the modem, the radio service, the data fallback and the bridge the apps use). Nothing starts before provisioning, and the Mobile data tile says why the modem is off when it is (a boot after a hang, a phone not provisioned).
- **Voice input, ready out of the box:** a voice bar on the keyboard with live words as you speak, on-device speech recognition, and a setting for the longest recording. Voxtype and its English speech model (Whisper base.en) come in the image, so dictation works from the first login with no setup step and no download, offline too.
- **Media controls** with album art in the notification shade and on the lock screen.
- **Lock screen:** a glance view (time, date, weather, notifications), the battery level, and a clock that follows the bar's 12/24-hour setting.
- **Fluid home and drawer gestures**, pill gestures, and hiding or showing the keyboard from the home pill.
- **Keyboard:** themed, with an action row in every layout and a language sheet from the globe key.
- **Updates** entry in the app drawer, with an update sheet that shows what's new and the phone's patches.
- **Louder speakers** under the protection firmware, and an earpiece mode for calls.
- **The louder speaker level from the first start, on phones with the verified firmware:** if the speaker-protection firmware `tools/phone-provision` copies from your factory image is the one verified on the reference phone (today that is factory image cp2a.260705.006; Google's files, byte for byte), the speakers run at the louder level right after the install, with no verification step on your phone. The watchdog still checks your phone at its first song (the protection running on your phone's own calibration, plausible coil temperatures) and falls back to the quieter level if anything looks wrong. Phones on other factory images start at the quieter everyday level until their firmware is verified. `omamobile-speaker-protection status` says which.
- **Files** opens folders with a single tap; the file chooser no longer stalls at login.
- **Fingerprint unlock after provisioning:** the phone makes its read-only copies of the calibration and `persist:/ss` itself, and setup offers "Add a fingerprint" after the PIN.
- **Screenshots and images:** tapping a screenshot's notification opens it in a touch image viewer (pinch and double-tap to zoom, swipe to the next, copy, share or delete), which is also the default for every image.

## Known issues

- **Calls, texts and mobile data work on the tested network, with the carrier settings set by hand.** Tested on the reference phone with one Verizon-network SIM (Tracfone): VoLTE calls both ways, texts over IMS, and mobile data as a fallback route behind Wi-Fi ([DEVICES](../DEVICES.md)). Other networks are untested. What still needs a hand: the SIM's APNs and the carrier manifest go into `/etc/omamobile/modem-sit.conf` (automatic APNs are [#373](https://github.com/derekross/omarchy-mobile/pull/373)), and until they are set the modem boots and registers nothing. Call audio through the earpiece needs the speakers at stage `media` (`omamobile-speaker-protection`): the louder stage and the earpiece exclude each other for now. With the modem up the phone never sleeps (the stack holds a sleep inhibitor, because suspend with the modem up isn't qualified), and each restart with the modem up costs one recovered storage error-handler pass. After a hang or a forced reset with the modem up, the next boot leaves the modem off once (the Mobile data tile says "Off after a hang or reset"; `journalctl -b -1 -u omamobile-modem` has that boot's line) and the boot after starts it again; if that boot ends the same way, the modem stays off (the tile adds "(held)") until `sudo omamobile-modem-start --retry`, and a clean reboot in between resets the count. `sudo touch /etc/omamobile/modem-hold` holds it from the first time. To keep the whole stack off: `sudo systemctl mask omamobile-modem-boot.target` (`disable` doesn't remove the package's link, and `systemctl is-enabled` reads "disabled" either way).
- **Suspend: works when asked for, auto-sleep off by default.** `systemctl suspend` sleeps and wakes reliably (on a key or the RTC), Wi-Fi stays connected across it, and standby lasts about 16 hours, well short of stock's. Auto-sleep after the screen goes off ships **off**; it can be turned on with `omarchy-toggle omamobile-sleep on`, but it hasn't finished a day-long test. With the modem up the phone doesn't sleep at all (above). Wi-Fi traffic and a USB plug can't wake the phone.
- **Cameras:** basic image quality (no Google ISP or tuning); the telephoto doesn't focus.
- **Bluetooth:** headset calls (HFP) don't work yet.
- **USB-C:** the phone is a USB device only (no host mode or role switch yet); after a replug, `omamobile-usb-rescue` reconnects it.
- **Fingerprint starts once per boot by design:** if it stops, restart the phone. Its first start writes the phone's secure storage (Trusty's area in the RPMB, the same kind of write as an Android factory reset); `persist` and `mfg_data` are only read. Fingers are enrolled again after a reinstall. To keep it off, `sudo systemctl mask omamobile-fp`.
- **Google's firmware is not included** and never will be: after installing, the bundle's `tools/phone-provision` extracts it from your own factory image for your phone (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, haptics, speakers, cameras).

## Phones already running a development build

A phone installed before this release (from the repository, not this bundle) needs two things once before `omarchy update` works again:

- **The new keyring.** Packages are now signed with the Omarchy Mobile release key, which keyrings before `omamobile-keyring` 20261026 don't know. Until it's installed, pacman refuses the `[omamobile]` repository ("key DDBA4581E0178D33DB69973F7F1041BF47291504 is unknown"). Install it as a local file, which pacman takes without the key: download `omamobile-keyring-20261026-1-any.pkg.tar.zst` from `https://pkgs.omamobile.org/stable/aarch64/` and run `sudo env OMARCHY_ALLOW_DIRECT_PACMAN=1 pacman -U` on it (Omarchy's update guard otherwise asks you to use `omarchy update`). Then check that `pacman-key --list-keys DDBA4581E0178D33DB69973F7F1041BF47291504` names the Omarchy Mobile Release Key, under the fingerprint at the end of these notes.
- **Hyprland 0.57 together with its libraries.** Arch Linux ARM moved to new Hyprland libraries on 2026-11-02, and the older Hyprland can't be installed next to them. `omarchy update` brings `hyprland` 0.57.0-3.1 (our build, with the DPMS fix) and `omamobile-shell` 0.2.79 in the same update; take both, then restart the phone. Without shell 0.2.79, Hyprland 0.57 draws the shell's transparent layers black (a black home screen).

The kernel stays as it is: on a development install, `omamobile-boot-update` is off unless `/etc/omamobile/boot-update.conf` says `AUTO_WRITE=yes`, so slot B is still written by `bin/omamobile push`.

## Installing

Follow [docs/INSTALL.md](../INSTALL.md), step by step. In short: a Pixel 7 Pro with an **unlocked bootloader**, a Linux computer with `fastboot` and the tools INSTALL.md lists, and Google's factory image for `cheetah`. Download the one file `omamobile-cheetah-2026.MM.DD.tar` with its `.tar.sha256` (and `.tar.sha256.sig`, to check the signature) from `https://pkgs.omamobile.org/releases/cheetah/2026.MM.DD/`, check it with `sha256sum -c`, unpack it with `tar -xf`, then run `bash install --dry-run` and `bash install` in the unpacked folder. After the first start, back up the phone's radio partitions with the bundle's `bash tools/nv-backup <folder>` (INSTALL.md step 6; read-only, keep the folder private: it holds your IMEI), then add Google's firmware with `bash tools/phone-provision --nv-backup <folder> <factory image>` (INSTALL.md step 7).

If the installer stops about the bootloader slots, don't work around it: [open an issue](https://github.com/derekross/omarchy-mobile/issues).

What it writes: slot B's `boot`, `init_boot`, `vendor_boot`, `vendor_kernel_boot` and `dtbo`, and `userdata` (**erased**). It never touches slot A, the bootloader, radio, `persist`, `efs`, `modem`, `devinfo` or `vbmeta`.

## Going back to stock Android

Stock Android is still on slot A. In fastboot: `fastboot --set-active=a`, then `fastboot reboot`, and factory-reset Android when it asks (its data was erased by the install). Or flash Google's factory image for `cheetah`. Before you first boot stock again, note that stock's modem stack rewrites `efs`, `persist` and `modem_userdata`; keep your read-only backup of them.

## Checksums and signatures

The bundle's `SHA256SUMS` is signed with the Omarchy Mobile release key (`2056 7593 566E EEE2 DD88  6460 8632 C807 5EB9 4673`); its `MANIFEST` and `manifest.json` name the root image and its SHA-256, so the installer checks the root image against the signed record before it contacts the phone.
